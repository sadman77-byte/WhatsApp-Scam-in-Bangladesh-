# Cybercrime Threat & Incident Analysis: WhatsApp Screen Sharing Fraud and Banking Credential Exposure

**Source Reference:** [ATN News – WhatsApp Screen Sharing Fraud Report](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xI4uf3paSu8)  
**Published Date:** November 23, 2026  
**Advisory / Organization Highlighted:** One Card  

---

## 1. Executive Summary
Cyber threat actors are exploiting the screen-sharing capability in WhatsApp alongside social engineering lures to compromise users' banking credentials, sensitive messages, and financial accounts [00:00:05], [00:00:34], [00:01:56]. By impersonating bank or trusted financial institution representatives and creating a false sense of urgency, attackers induce victims to enable screen sharing or video calls, exposing on-screen activity such as passwords, account numbers, and OTPs in real time [00:00:59], [00:01:10], [00:01:44].

---

## 2. Classification of Cybercrime
Based on the attack flow and threat indicators described in the report, this activity falls under **Cyber-Enabled Financial Fraud**, driven by:
1. **Social Engineering & Impersonation (Vishing / Pretexting):** Attackers pose as bank or financial institution officials and fabricate urgent account problems to pressure victims into acting quickly without verification [00:00:59], [00:01:10], [00:03:05].
2. **Real-Time Screen Surveillance / Unauthorized Information Disclosure:** Abusing messaging screen-sharing features once authorized by the user to observe sensitive app screens, credentials, OTPs, and private messages [00:01:10], [00:01:20], [00:01:44].
3. **Malware / Keylogger Risk (Secondary Vector):** In certain cases, attackers may also attempt to deploy harmful software such as keyloggers or push untrusted apps/permissions to expand access [00:02:07], [00:02:42].

---

## 3. Attack Workflow & Techniques Highlighted
* **Impersonation & Urgency Lure:** Fraudsters call claiming to be from a bank or financial service provider and warn that the victim's account faces an urgent issue with potential loss of all funds unless resolved immediately [00:00:59], [00:01:10].
* **Coerced Screen Sharing / Video Call:** Under pressure, the victim is prompted to start WhatsApp screen sharing or a video call [00:01:10], [00:01:20].
* **Visual Harvesting of Sensitive Data:** Once screen sharing is active, anything displayed on the phone screen becomes visible to the caller—including passwords, account numbers, OTPs when accessing banking apps, and personal messages [00:01:20], [00:01:44], [00:01:53].
* **Unauthorized Transactions & Account Takeover:** Captured credentials and sensitive data create a high risk of unauthorized financial transactions or takeover of the bank account, sometimes accompanied by attempts to use malicious software like keyloggers [00:01:56], [00:02:07].

---

## 4. Potential Impact
* **Financial Loss:** Unauthorized transfers or draining of funds from the victim's bank account [00:00:01], [00:01:56].
* **Account Compromise:** Loss of control over banking access and exposure of confidential personal communications/data [00:01:53], [00:01:56].

---

## 5. Defensive Recommendations & Mitigations
* **Verify Caller Identity Independently:** Do not trust incoming calls claiming to be from a bank or financial institution at face value; call the bank's official number to verify [00:02:16], [00:02:27].
* **Refuse Screen Sharing on Request:** Never turn on screen sharing at the request of unknown or unverified callers [00:02:32].
* **Avoid Banking During Screen Sharing:** Do not open or use banking apps while screen sharing is enabled [00:02:36].
* **Restrict App Installations & Permissions:** Avoid installing apps from untrusted sources and be cautious when granting permissions [00:02:42].
* **Never Share OTPs or Passwords & Resist Urgency Pressure:** Banks do not call to ask for OTPs or passwords; do not share sensitive information with anyone—known or unknown—and verify identity before acting when pressured by urgency claims [00:02:53], [00:03:05].
